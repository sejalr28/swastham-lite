# Napping: When It Helps and When It Hurts

Short naps can improve alertness, mood, and performance, particularly for people who are sleep-deprived or working irregular hours. A commonly cited guideline is to keep naps to around 20 to 30 minutes, which helps avoid entering deep sleep stages that can cause grogginess (sleep inertia) upon waking.

Nap timing also matters. Naps taken in the early-to-mid afternoon are less likely to interfere with nighttime sleep than naps taken in the late afternoon or evening, since the latter reduce accumulated "sleep pressure" close to the intended bedtime.

For people with ongoing difficulty falling asleep at night or diagnosed insomnia, sleep specialists often recommend reducing or eliminating naps, since naps can further reduce nighttime sleep drive and worsen the cycle of poor nighttime sleep.

As with other sleep hygiene topics, individual response varies, and these are general patterns rather than fixed rules for every person.
