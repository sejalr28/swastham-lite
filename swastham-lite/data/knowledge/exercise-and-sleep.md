# Physical Activity and Sleep

Regular physical activity is generally associated with better sleep quality, including falling asleep faster and spending more time in deep sleep, compared to a sedentary lifestyle.

Timing can matter for some individuals. Vigorous exercise very close to bedtime raises core body temperature and heart rate, which can delay sleep onset for people who are sensitive to this effect, since falling asleep is associated with a drop in core temperature. For those individuals, finishing intense workouts at least a few hours before bed is a common suggestion.

That said, the relationship is individual: many people report no negative effect from evening exercise, and for some, a light evening walk or gentle stretching can actually support relaxation before bed. Morning or afternoon exercise, combined with outdoor light exposure, may offer an additional circadian benefit by reinforcing daytime alertness cues.

Consistency of activity over time appears to matter more for sleep benefits than the exact time of day it is performed.
