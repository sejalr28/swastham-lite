# Keeping a Consistent Sleep Schedule

Going to bed and waking up at roughly the same time every day, including weekends, is one of the most consistently recommended sleep hygiene practices. This regularity helps stabilize the body's internal circadian clock, which governs the timing of sleepiness and alertness across a 24-hour period.

Large swings in wake time between weekdays and weekends, sometimes called "social jet lag," can make it harder to fall asleep at the intended time and can contribute to grogginess and reduced alertness during the week.

Morning light exposure shortly after waking helps reinforce a consistent circadian rhythm. Natural daylight is generally more effective for this purpose than indoor lighting, which is typically far dimmer than outdoor light even on an overcast day.

For people whose schedules genuinely vary, such as shift workers, maintaining consistency around the sleep period itself (rather than the clock time) and protecting a dark, quiet sleep window are more realistic goals than fixing a single bedtime.
