# Optimizing the Bedroom Environment

The physical sleep environment influences both how quickly a person falls asleep and how many times they wake during the night, even if those brief awakenings aren't remembered in the morning.

Temperature is one of the more impactful factors. A cool room, generally in the range often cited as comfortable by sleep researchers, tends to support sleep better than a warm one, because core body temperature naturally drops as part of the process of falling asleep.

Darkness matters because light, especially blue-spectrum light, suppresses the release of melatonin, a hormone involved in signaling sleep onset. Blackout curtains, dimming devices before bed, and covering small standby lights on electronics can reduce this effect.

Noise disruption can fragment sleep even without fully waking a person. Consistent background sound (a fan, white noise machine, or earplugs) can help mask intermittent noises like traffic or a partner's movement.

Reserving the bed primarily for sleep, rather than working or scrolling on a phone while lying in it, helps the brain build a strong association between the bed and sleep rather than the bed and wakeful activity.
