# Stress, Racing Thoughts, and Sleep

Stress and an overactive mind at bedtime are among the most commonly reported barriers to falling asleep. Physiologically, stress activates the sympathetic nervous system, raising heart rate and alertness, which works against the relaxed state needed for sleep onset.

Several general wind-down strategies are commonly suggested to reduce pre-sleep stress: a consistent, calming pre-bed routine (such as reading, light stretching, or a warm shower); writing down tomorrow's to-do list or worries earlier in the evening so they don't surface at bedtime; and simple breathing exercises that slow respiration and activate the parasympathetic nervous system.

Getting out of bed if unable to sleep after roughly 20 minutes, and returning only when sleepy, is a widely recommended practice. It helps prevent the bed from becoming associated with frustration and wakefulness rather than rest.

Occasional stress-related sleep disruption is common and usually not a cause for concern on its own. However, persistent sleep difficulty accompanied by ongoing low mood, anxiety, or an inability to function during the day is a signal to speak with a healthcare professional rather than relying solely on self-help strategies.
