# Caffeine, Alcohol, and Sleep Quality

Caffeine is a stimulant with a half-life that commonly falls in the range of about five to six hours in healthy adults, though this varies by individual metabolism. This means a substantial portion of the caffeine from an afternoon coffee can still be active in the body at bedtime. A common general guideline is to avoid caffeine intake in the 6 to 8 hours before the intended bedtime, though sensitive individuals may need a longer window.

Alcohol has a more complex relationship with sleep. It can shorten the time it takes to fall asleep because of its sedative effect, but it tends to disrupt sleep architecture later in the night, reducing REM sleep and increasing awakenings as the body metabolizes it. As a result, sleep after alcohol consumption is often lighter and less restorative even if total sleep time seems normal.

Nicotine is also a stimulant and has been associated with lighter, more fragmented sleep and reduced total sleep time in regular users.

This information describes general population-level patterns. It is not guidance on substance use, dependence, or cessation, and it is not a recommendation regarding alcohol, caffeine, or nicotine consumption for any specific individual.
